# Workflow 03 — The Status Email That Stops "Any Updates?" Pings

> **Who it's for:** anyone whose manager or client pings "any updates?" more than once a week.
> **Time:** 15 min of anxiety → 4 lines, 3 min. **Any chat LLM.**

---

## The insight nobody tells you

"Any updates?" isn't a request for information. It's **an anxiety leak from someone who can't see what you're doing.** You can't answer anxiety with facts — you answer it with *rhythm*.

A predictable broadcast on a schedule kills the pings better than instant replies ever will.

## Step 1 — Collect your fragments

This week's tickets, messages, numbers — raw dump, same as Workflow 01.

## Step 2 — Paste this prompt above the dump

```
You are my status email writer. Rules:

1. Four lines, this exact structure:
   DONE: [1-2 items, each with a number]
   NEXT: [1-2 items, each with a date]
   RISKS: [only real blockers, each with what I need to unblock it]
   NOTHING TO WORRY ABOUT: [one true sentence]

2. Every progress claim must map to evidence in my dump. No evidence
   → downgrade the verb ("started," never "nearly done").

3. No reassurance adjectives ("smoothly," "well under way," "under
   control") unless backed by a number in the same line.

4. If I gave you zero risks, write exactly: "No blockers I can see —
   I'll flag by [day] if that changes." Do not invent one to seem
   thorough.
```

## Step 3 — The 60-second audit: the reassurance sweep

AI's instinct is to comfort. That's exactly backwards — **comfort words are what get you caught.** "Going smoothly" with no number reads as a cover-up to anyone who's been burned before.

Scan every line: any reassurance word without a number in the same line → cut it or attach a number. Then the **downgrade check**: AI upgrades verbs silently (*started → progressing → nearly done*). Compare each DONE verb against your raw dump — say what happened, not what it's becoming.

## Known failure modes

| The AI will try to… | Your fix |
|---|---|
| Add warmth/inflation it thinks sounds professional | Reassurance sweep above |
| Bury the one real risk mid-line-3 where nobody reads | RISKS gets its own line, max one clause each |
| Invent a deadline for NEXT items to look decisive | Dates only from your dump |

## Why this works

The fourth line is the actual anti-ping weapon. "Nothing to worry about" — when true — is the sentence your manager forwards to *their* manager, and the reason they stop checking on you. Anxiety answered with structure, once, on a schedule.
