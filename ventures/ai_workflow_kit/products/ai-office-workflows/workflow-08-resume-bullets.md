# Workflow 08 — Resume Bullet Rewriter (What Did You *Actually* Do?)

> **Who it's for:** anyone updating a resume, a LinkedIn profile, or a promotion packet.
> **Time:** 3 hours → 40 min. **Any chat LLM.**

---

## The insight nobody tells you

Weak bullets describe **responsibilities**. Strong bullets describe **consequences** — what changed because you were there.

The rewrite isn't prose dressing. It's an **evidence hunt**: for every thing you "were responsible for," there's a number somewhere — how many, how much, how often, how long. Your memory of those numbers is the moat; the AI's vocabulary is not.

## Step 1 — Paste your current bullets

As-is, including the embarrassing "responsible for" ones. Especially those.

## Step 2 — Paste this prompt above them

```
You are my resume bullet editor. Rules:

1. Rewrite each bullet as: VERB + what I did + measurable result +
   who it helped.

2. You may NOT invent metrics. If I gave no number, write [NUMBER?]
   and ask me: "approximately how many / how much / how often?"

3. Ban list: "responsible for," "helped with," "involved in,"
   "assisted," "supported." These are job descriptions, not
   achievements.

4. Keep every claim at the evidence level I gave. "Owned the monthly
   report" stays "owned" — never upgrade it to "streamlined reporting
   across the team."

5. Output format per bullet:
   ORIGINAL → REWRITE → the one question I must answer to make it
   stronger.
```

## Step 3 — The 60-second audit: the number-less achievement sweep

Scan the rewrites: **every bullet either contains a real number you provided, or ends in [NUMBER?].** If any bullet sounds impressive with neither, the AI upgraded you beyond your evidence.

That bullet isn't a win — it's an **interview trap.** Interviewers probe exactly the shiniest claims; a bullet you can't defend costs more than one you never wrote.

## Known failure modes

| The AI will try to… | Your fix |
|---|---|
| Invent plausible metrics ("reduced processing time by 40%") | The [NUMBER?] rule — never accept a number you didn't supply |
| Inflate your role ("worked on" → "led") | Evidence-level rule; read every verb literally |
| Make everything sound equally epic | Epic everything = epic nothing; keep small things small |

## Why this works

The [NUMBER?] questions are the actual product: they turn resume-writing from prose editing into an **evidence inventory**. Answer twenty of them and you have your interview answers, your promotion case, and your self-review — same numbers, three documents.
